## How it was tested

Return to Player (RTP) is the share of everything staked that a game pays back over the long run. It was measured by **actually playing the game** — not by reading a number out of a configuration file.

**Procedure**

- Every engine the backend registers was driven through its real round function, at a flat stake, using the same cryptographic random source the live game uses.
- Total returned ÷ total staked gives the measured RTP. Hit rate (share of winning rounds) and the largest win observed were recorded at the same time.
- Millions of rounds are needed because slot-style games are high-variance: a small sample cannot pin down the true long-run figure.

**How pass/fail was decided**

- GLI-19 §4.7.1 requires a theoretical payout of **at least 75%**. Measuring below that floor is a failure.
- The precision figure (±) is a 3-standard-error range: the tighter it is, the more firmly the sample pins the true RTP. A wide range is reported as provisional rather than certified.
- An engine that returns **no payouts at all** cannot be certified: its payout math was not measurable, which is reported as a failure rather than a pass.
